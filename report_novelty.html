How much of the CoFold Arena Ab–Ag leaderboard comes from targets models have effectively seen?

For each model with a documented training cutoff, every one of the 422 targets is searched against the whole PDB as it stood before that cutoff. Two things are checked: whether a homologous antigen was already deposited, and whether the same antibody–antigen complex was (same antigen at ≥90% identity, plus a heavy chain with the same CDR-H3 and IGHV family). Companion to report.html (homology reweighting).

Short answer: very little of the leaderboard comes from targets the models have effectively seen, and the top of the ranking holds without them.

Antigen-level overlap is nearly universal and doesn't help. 320 of 422 targets (76%) had an antigen at ≥90% identity in the PDB before 2021-09-30, the cutoff 12 of the 18 models share; only 71 (17%) were below 50%. Scores on those familiar antigens are no higher than on novel ones. Mol-1 averages 0.356 on ≥90% antigens and 0.340 on <50%; OpenDDE 0.352 vs 0.323. Re-scoring each model only on its own novel targets keeps Mol-1, OpenDDE, Protenix-v2 and OpenDDE-AbAg as the top four, with Mol-1 #1 (rank interval 1–4). The mid-table reshuffles (Kendall τ 0.75), but those intervals are wide because each model keeps only about 71 targets.

Complex-level leakage is real but small. For a 2021-09-30 model, 17 targets (4%) are an antibody–antigen complex already in the PDB: rituximab–CD20, Nb35–Gs, the GFP enhancer nanobody, a BG505 SOSIP complex, and others. Top models score about 0.62–0.69 on them against about 0.35 elsewhere, but there are too few to move a mean much. Removing them lowers a 2021-cutoff model's score by at most 0.014 DockQ, Boltz-2's by 0.020, and Protenix-v1-20250630's by 0.031.

The Protenix natural experiment puts a number on seeing the target itself. The 2025-cutoff checkpoint beats the 2021 one by +0.060 DockQ on the 163 targets that were in its training set, and by only +0.009 (CI −0.019 to +0.038) on the 259 released after its cutoff. That difference of +0.051 (CI +0.003 to +0.100) is the value of training on the exact structure; four more years of other PDB data adds about nothing measurable.

Mol-1's results are consistent with its 2021-09-30 cutoff in aggregate, with two individual targets worth a closer look. Its margin over the other 2021-cutoff models is the same (+0.12) on novel targets, on antigens first deposited after 2021, and on antigens deposited before. On the 9 targets whose complex was first deposited after 2021-09-30, its margin is larger (+0.32), but the CI on the excess spans zero. Two of the nine are cases where Mol-1 alone is accurate: KRas (8VGQ) and RSV F with Fab 5B11 (8WZ5). In the RSV case the earlier copy (8WZ4) is itself an Arena target released in 2024-11, and Mol-1 is also uniquely accurate there. These patterns fit a strong model as well as later data exposure, and nine targets can't separate the two.

Antibody redundancy stays negligible. Clonotype grouping links only 13 targets, and the joint antigen + clonotype weighting reproduces the published ranking exactly (τ = 1.0). The old VH-90% clusters were mostly synthetic-library Fabs sharing an IGHV3-23 or IGHV3-66 framework with unrelated CDR-H3s and antigens.

Method and assumptions

  • PDB reference. wwPDB pdb_seqres.txt.gz (all protein chains, collapsed to unique sequences) and the initial release date of every current entry from the RCSB holdings list plus GraphQL. Snapshot: 2026-11-02. Obsolete entries are not included, so a structure that was superseded before 2026 counts as never deposited.
  • "Before cutoff" means a PDB initial release date strictly earlier than the model's cutoff date. Models trained on a time split may also have filtered by deposition date or used distillation sets; release date is the closest public proxy.
  • Antigen identity. mmseqs easy-search -s 7.5 -e 1e-3 --max-seqs 20000 of each target antigen chain against all unique PDB protein sequences. Identity is MMseqs2 fident over the aligned region. A hit counts only if it covers ≥50% of the target chain, with coverage measured on the unmasked part of the chain. Peptide antigens (<20 aa) are also matched by exact substring search. For a multi-chain antigen the target's identity is the maximum over its chains, so a pMHC target counts as seen when its HLA heavy chain was, even if the peptide is new.
  • Own entry. The target's own PDB entry is always excluded from the search. The one exception is Protenix-v1-20250630: for the 163 targets released before 2025-06-30, its training set contained the target itself, so those targets are counted as seen (identity 1.0, seen complex) and flagged.
  • Seen complex. A pre-cutoff entry that contains (a) a chain at ≥90% identity over ≥80% of a target antigen chain and (b) a heavy chain with an equal-length CDR-H3 at ≥80% identity and the same IGHV family. CDRs use IMGT numbering (CDR-H3 = IMGT 105–117); germlines are assigned by ANARCI against human and mouse references, applied identically to target and PDB chains. This is a sequence proxy for "same epitope, same antibody", with no structural epitope comparison. Shark VNARs (2 targets) can't be numbered and are never flagged.
  • Statistics. Means are unweighted per-target DockQ, like the published leaderboard. CIs and rank intervals come from a paired cluster bootstrap over antigen-50% homology groups, B = 2,000, with the same resample used for every model. A model's novel-only score averages its own novel targets within each resample.
  • Clonotypes. Same IGHV gene (allele stripped, germline species included in the key), same CDR-H3 length, ≥80% CDR-H3 identity, single linkage. Germlines here are assigned against all ANARCI species including alpaca, so VHHs get camelid genes.
  • Fusion masking. Partner segments are found by MMseqs2 against UniProt P0AEX9 (MBP), P0ABE7 (cyt b562/BRIL), P00720 (T4L), P42212 (GFP), Q9U6Y8 (DsRed), P01857 (IgG1 constant) and P0AA25 (thioredoxin), at ≥50% identity over ≥50 residues, and replaced by X.
